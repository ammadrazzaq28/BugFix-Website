003 — Case study · Voxa

Rebuilding the
audio engine.

The version that shipped first worked. It just didn't sound good enough, and the reviews said so. Here's what we changed.

A voice changer that survives being listened to twice.

Problem

Naive pitch shifting moved the formants along with the pitch, so every effect landed in chipmunk territory. Exports also clipped the reverb tail, cutting the last half-second off every recording.

Approach

Split pitch, formant and tempo into three independent controls. Built a per-effect DSP recipe with explicit formant targets instead of one shared shifter, and moved everything into a custom in-process AUv3 audio unit.

Outcome

Sample-accurate LFO modulation, correct export tails, and effects that hold up on a second listen. Rating recovered inside two release cycles.

004 — What we do

Build it. Then
actually ship it.

Most studios hand you a binary and leave. Shipping is the part that decides whether the app makes money, so we stay for it.

01 — Build

Native, not a wrapper.

  • SwiftUI and UIKit on iOS, Kotlin and Jetpack Compose on Android
  • MVVM + Coordinators, so screens stay testable as the app grows
  • On-device AI and audio work: Core ML, AVAudioEngine, custom AUv3
  • Interactive HTML prototypes before a line of app code gets written
  • Widgets, extensions, keyboards, share sheets — the platform surface area
02 — Ship

Revenue is a feature.

  • StoreKit 2 and Play Billing, with paywalls we A/B test properly
  • Credit economies and subscription tiers that fit the product, not a template
  • Ad mediation across AdMob, AppLovin MAX, Meta Audience Network, ironSource
  • ATT, Data safety, Play policy declarations and rejection handling
  • Store listing, screenshots, ASO and the post-launch update cadence
What we don't do

The work we turn down is part of the product.

  • Wrappers. If it can ship as a WebView, we are the wrong studio.
  • Handoffs at binary. Launch day is a milestone, not the finish.
  • Template paywalls. Monetization has to fit the app, not a screenshot from another one.
005 — How a build runs

Five stages.
No surprises.

Order matters here — each stage produces something you can look at before we spend money on the next one.

Competitor teardown, monetization model, and a feature list with a line drawn through what ships in v1.

008 — Questions

You asked,
we answered.

How long does a build take?

Scope and prototype are weeks, not months. A native v1 with monetization usually lands in 10–16 weeks depending on platform surface area. You see a TestFlight or internal-test build every two weeks.

Do you only work on your own apps?

No. The portfolio is owned by us; the same team takes client work. The point of owning eight shipping apps is that you don't pay us to learn the stores on your budget.

Native, or a cross-platform wrapper?

Native. SwiftUI and UIKit on iOS, Kotlin and Jetpack Compose on Android. Widgets, keyboards, share sheets and on-device audio are not optional extras here.

Do you stay after launch?

Yes. Listing, ASO, review responses and the first three updates are part of ship. Launch day is not the finish.

Where are you based?

Lahore, Pakistan. We reply within one working day. If the work isn't a fit we'll say so instead of billing you to find out.
